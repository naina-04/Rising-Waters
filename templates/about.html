Understanding the Data

A guide to the meteorological factors that drive our flood prediction model.

Annual Rainfall

The total accumulation of rain a specific region receives over a full year, measured in millimeters (mm). Regions with historically high annual rainfall have a higher baseline risk, as their soil and drainage systems are frequently tested by large volumes of water.

Seasonal Rainfall

The amount of rain concentrated within a specific weather season (like a monsoon or wet season). A sudden, massive spike in seasonal rainfall is one of the most critical triggers for flash floods, as the ground becomes rapidly saturated.

Temperature

The current atmospheric temperature measured in Celsius (°C). Extreme temperatures can influence flood risks in multiple ways: high heat increases evaporation rates (fueling storm clouds), while sudden warming in colder regions can trigger rapid snowmelt.

Humidity

The concentration of water vapor present in the air, represented as a percentage. High humidity levels combined with specific temperature drops often lead to intense, prolonged precipitation events that overwhelm local drainage systems.

Cloud Visibility

A scaled metric (0.0 to 10.0) indicating atmospheric clarity and cloud cover density. Lower visibility scores often correlate with the presence of dense, moisture-heavy storm clouds, signaling an imminent threat of heavy rainfall.

Monsoon Intensity

A severity index ranging from 1 to 15 that represents the extreme nature of local weather patterns. A higher intensity score points toward torrential downpours, severe storm systems, and an exponentially higher probability of catastrophic flooding.
